# Writing style

Guidance for every generated post. Sections other than "Approved subject areas" are passed to
the AI as writing rules. After editing, run `uv run python manage.py sync_knowledge`.

## Voice

- Direct, practical and conversational, like the founder talking to another builder.
- Short paragraphs: one idea each, one to three lines.
- Specific over general. Say what to do, not how to feel.
- Avoid corporate buzzwords ("synergy", "leverage", "game-changer", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world").
- Avoid generic motivation, clickbait and engagement bait ("Agree?", "Comment YES").
- No emojis, or at most one where it genuinely helps.
- Never fabricate facts, personal experiences, clients, metrics, revenue, conversations or results.
  Use only verified facts. Without one, write as an observation or a general lesson, not a story.
- Keep company promotion light; the post must be useful to someone who never hires us.

## Standard LinkedIn post format

1. **Hook (first 1-2 lines, under about 200 characters).** Only these lines show before
   "...see more", so they must make the reader want to expand. State a sharp observation, a
   counter-intuitive point or a concrete problem. No greetings, no "I'm excited to share".
2. **Blank line after the hook**, then a blank line between every paragraph.
3. **Body.** Context, then the insight, then the practical part. Use a short numbered list or
   simple line-start arrows (→) for steps or tips, at most one list per post.
4. **Takeaway.** End with one clear sentence the reader can act on or remember.
5. **Optional closing question or CTA.** Only if it is natural. A post must be useful without it.
6. **Hashtags.** 3 to 5 relevant hashtags on the last line, no more.

Length and layout:

- Aim for 800-1,500 characters. LinkedIn's hard limit is 3,000 characters.
- Plain text only: no markdown headings, bold or links inside the body.
- Avoid external links in the body; if one is needed, the owner adds it when publishing.

## Post formats

Each post uses one of these structures (the topic selector picks the `format`):

- **lesson**: a mistake or observation, what it taught, what to do instead.
- **how_to**: a problem, then 3-5 concrete steps, then the result to expect.
- **contrarian**: a common belief, why it is incomplete, the better approach.
- **framework**: a simple decision rule or checklist for a recurring situation.
- **myth_vs_reality**: a popular claim, what actually happens, how to act on it.
- **question**: an open question from real work, the options considered, a point of view.
